MEL - Melanoma Clinical Guidelines
====================================
Source: Adapted from AAD/WHO dermatology guidelines (educational summary)

OVERVIEW
Melanoma is the most dangerous form of skin cancer, arising from melanocytes.
Early detection is critical — 5-year survival exceeds 98% if caught at stage I,
but drops to ~25% at stage IV.

ABCDE RULE FOR DETECTION
- Asymmetry: One half unlike the other
- Border: Ragged, notched, or blurred edges
- Color: Variation in color (tan, brown, black, red, white, or blue)
- Diameter: Larger than 6mm (pencil eraser size)
- Evolving: Any change in size, shape, color, or new symptom (bleeding, itching)

RED FLAGS REQUIRING URGENT REFERRAL
- Rapidly growing lesion (weeks, not months)
- Spontaneous bleeding or ulceration
- Irregular or notched border
- Itching AND pain simultaneously present
- Lesion diameter > 6mm with color variation

CLINICAL MANAGEMENT
- Urgent dermatology referral within 2 weeks (suspected melanoma pathway)
- Dermoscopy evaluation by trained clinician
- Excision biopsy is the gold standard for definitive diagnosis
- Sentinel lymph node biopsy for lesions > 1mm Breslow thickness
- Do NOT apply topical treatments before biopsy — it alters histology

PATIENT ADVICE
- Avoid sun exposure; use SPF 50+ sunscreen daily
- Perform monthly self-skin checks
- Monitor lesion for ANY change in size, shape, colour, or symptoms
- Do not delay seeking evaluation if the lesion changes

DISCLAIMER
This is decision-support information only. A qualified dermatologist must
confirm any diagnosis. Do not tell patients they are cancer-free based on
AI output alone. Always use: "Monitor and consult a doctor if it changes."

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
MEL must be distinguished from: NV (benign nevus), BKL (seborrhoeic keratosis),
BCC (basal cell carcinoma — also malignant but rarely metastasises), DF (dermatofibroma).
