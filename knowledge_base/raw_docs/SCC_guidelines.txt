SCC - Squamous Cell Carcinoma Clinical Guidelines
==================================================
Source: Adapted from AAD/NICE dermatology guidelines (educational summary)

OVERVIEW
Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is the second most common skin cancer, arising from
squamous cells of the epidermis. Unlike BCC, SCC has a meaningful metastasis risk
(~2-5% overall; higher in immunocompromised patients or high-risk sites like lips/ears).

CLINICAL FEATURES
- Firm red nodule or flat lesion with scaly, crusted surface
- New sore or raised area on an old scar
- Rough, scaly patch on the lip
- Red sore or rough patch inside the mouth
- Wart-like growth
- Commonly on sun-exposed areas; also mucous membranes and genitals

RED FLAGS FOR SCC
- Rapid growth over weeks
- Ulceration or spontaneous bleeding
- Perineural invasion (numbness, tingling around lesion)
- Lesion on lip, ear, or in immunocompromised patient
- Regional lymph node enlargement

RELATIONSHIP TO ACTINIC KERATOSIS (AK)
- AK is the precursor lesion to SCC
- ~10% of untreated AKs may progress to SCC over 10 years
- AK management reduces SCC incidence

MANAGEMENT
- Surgical excision: standard treatment with clear margins
- Mohs surgery: for high-risk SCC (face, recurrent, large, perineural)
- Radiation therapy: for inoperable cases or post-excision adjuvant
- Sentinel node biopsy: for high-risk SCC (>2cm, >2mm depth, immunocompromised)
- Cemiplimab / pembrolizumab: immunotherapy for advanced/metastatic SCC

PATIENT ADVICE
- Immediate dermatology referral for any suspected SCC
- Sun protection: SPF 50+, protective clothing year-round
- Stop smoking (risk factor for oral/lip SCC)
- Monitor and consult a doctor if the lesion changes in size, colour, or texture

DISCLAIMER
This is decision-support information only. A dermatologist must confirm diagnosis.
Never tell a patient they are cancer-free based on AI screening output alone.

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
SCC vs: AK (precursor — treatment prevents progression), BCC (slower, no metastasis),
MEL (melanoma — higher systemic risk), VASC (vascular lesion — different management).
