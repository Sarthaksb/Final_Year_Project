NV - Melanocytic Nevus (Common Mole) Clinical Guidelines
=========================================================
Source: Adapted from AAD dermatology guidelines (educational summary)

OVERVIEW
Melanocytic nevi (common moles) are benign proliferations of melanocytes. They are
extremely common — most adults have 10–40 moles. The vast majority are entirely benign,
but any changing mole warrants clinical evaluation.

CLINICAL FEATURES (BENIGN CHARACTERISTICS)
- Symmetric shape (both halves match)
- Smooth, well-defined border
- Uniform single color (tan, brown, or flesh-toned)
- Diameter usually < 6mm
- Stable over years — NOT evolving

WHEN A NEVUS MAY BECOME CONCERNING
- Asymmetry develops
- Border becomes irregular, notched, or blurred
- Color changes or becomes variegated (multiple colors)
- Diameter increases beyond 6mm
- Evolving: any change in size, shape, color, or new symptoms (bleeding, itching, pain)
- A new mole appearing after age 50

TYPES OF NEVI
- Common acquired nevus: most prevalent; develops in childhood/adolescence
- Congenital nevus: present at birth; large ones have slightly higher MEL risk
- Dysplastic (atypical) nevus: irregular features; marker of elevated MEL risk
- Blue nevus: blue-grey color; benign but can mimic melanoma

MANAGEMENT
- Most benign nevi require no treatment
- Excision biopsy if: changing, symptomatic, cosmetically bothersome, or diagnostic doubt
- Dysplastic nevi: excise if severely atypical; regular surveillance otherwise
- High nevus count (>50) is a melanoma risk factor — annual skin checks recommended

PATIENT ADVICE
- Perform monthly self-examination
- Photograph baseline moles and compare at 3-month intervals
- Any change in a mole should prompt dermatology evaluation
- Monitor and consult a doctor if it changes
- Use SPF 50+ sunscreen — UV exposure promotes nevus evolution

DISCLAIMER
This AI tool provides decision support only. A dermatologist must evaluate any
changing or symptomatic mole. Do not interpret "NV" prediction as confirmation
that no further evaluation is needed.

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
NV vs: MEL (melanoma — must rule out with ABCDE), BKL (seborrhoeic keratosis —
stuck-on appearance), DF (dermatofibroma — firm, dimples with pinch).
