BCC - Basal Cell Carcinoma Clinical Guidelines
===============================================
Source: Adapted from AAD/NICE dermatology guidelines (educational summary)

OVERVIEW
Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is the most common skin cancer worldwide, arising from
basal cells of the epidermis. It grows slowly and rarely metastasises, but causes
significant local tissue destruction if left untreated. Early treatment is curative.

CLINICAL FEATURES
- Pearly or waxy bump, often with visible blood vessels (telangiectasia)
- Flat, flesh-coloured or brown scar-like lesion
- Bleeding or scabbing sore that heals and returns
- Pink growth with raised edges and a crusted centre
- Commonly on sun-exposed areas: face, ears, neck, scalp, shoulders

RISK FACTORS
- Cumulative UV exposure (sun, tanning beds)
- Fair skin, light eyes/hair, freckles
- Personal or family history of BCC or other skin cancers
- Immunosuppression (organ transplant recipients have 65x higher risk)
- Radiation therapy history

MANAGEMENT
- Surgical excision: primary treatment; high cure rates (>95%)
- Mohs micrographic surgery: for high-risk or recurrent BCC
- Topical treatments (imiquimod, 5-FU): for superficial BCC only
- Photodynamic therapy: for superficial BCC in cosmetically sensitive areas
- Hedgehog pathway inhibitors (vismodegib): for advanced/metastatic BCC

MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP
- Annual skin checks after first BCC — 50% risk of second BCC within 5 years
- Full-body skin examination by dermatologist
- Patient self-examination monthly

PATIENT ADVICE
- Strict sun protection (SPF 50+, protective clothing, avoid peak UV hours)
- Report any new growths, sores that don't heal, or lesion changes promptly
- Monitor and consult a doctor if the lesion changes in any way

DISCLAIMER
This is decision-support information only. A qualified dermatologist must confirm
any diagnosis. AI output should never state "no cancer" or "you are fine."

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
BCC vs: SCC (squamous cell — grows faster, higher metastasis risk), MEL (melanoma),
BKL (seborrhoeic keratosis — benign), VASC (vascular lesion), AK (actinic keratosis).
